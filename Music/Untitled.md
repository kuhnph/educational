## Quick Reference

### Consonant Intervals
- **0** – Perfect Unison (P1)
- **3** – Minor 3rd (m3)
- **4** – Major 3rd (M3)
- **7** – Perfect 5th (P5)
- **8** – Minor 6th (m6)
- **9** – Major 6th (M6)
- **12** – Perfect Octave (P8)

### Dissonant Intervals
- **1** – Minor 2nd (m2)
- **2** – Major 2nd (M2)
- **6** – Tritone (A4/d5)
- **10** – Minor 7th (m7)
- **11** – Major 7th (M7)

### Special Case
- **5** – Perfect 4th (P4)

> **Note:** The Perfect 4th is context-dependent. By itself (especially melodically), it is generally heard as consonant. When it appears above the bass in traditional classical harmony, it is often treated as a dissonance that resolves.

## Memory Aid

- **Perfect intervals:** 1, 4, 5, 8
- **Major/Minor intervals:** 2, 3, 6, 7
- **Major and minor triads are built entirely from consonant intervals.**
- **Dissonant intervals create tension and often resolve to consonant intervals.**
