# ACQ 1010 Acquisition Fundamentals

## Overview

The Department of Defense (DoD) acquisition process exists to transform operational needs into fielded capabilities while balancing **cost, schedule, performance, supportability, and risk** throughout the entire system life cycle. Acquisition is not simply purchasing equipment—it is an integrated systems engineering and management process that begins with identifying capability gaps and continues through development, production, sustainment, and eventual disposal.

---

# The Defense Acquisition Life Cycle

The acquisition life cycle is divided into several phases, each with distinct objectives.

## Material Solution Analysis (MSA)

The Material Solution Analysis phase evaluates potential concepts for satisfying an operational need.

Primary objectives include:

- identifying feasible concepts
- performing early trade studies
- identifying initial product support requirements
- beginning sustainment planning
- assessing affordability

---

## Technology Maturation and Risk Reduction (TMRR)

This phase reduces technical risk before committing to full development.

Activities include:

- technology maturation
- systems engineering
- prototype development
- engineering integration
- early testing
- life-cycle cost risk reduction

---

## Engineering and Manufacturing Development (EMD)

EMD transforms the selected concept into a complete system design.

Major objectives include:

- detailed system design
- subsystem integration
- manufacturing process development
- developmental testing
- verification that requirements have been met

---

## Production and Deployment

Once a design has been verified, the system enters production.

Objectives include:

- manufacturing
- operational testing
- fielding
- deployment to operational units

Production funding primarily comes from **Procurement appropriations**.

---

## Operations and Support

This is typically the longest phase of the acquisition life cycle.

Focus areas include:

- maintenance
- logistics
- upgrades
- sustainment
- readiness
- disposal planning

The majority of total ownership costs occur during this phase.

---

# Systems Engineering

Systems engineering provides the technical foundation for acquisition by translating operational needs into measurable engineering requirements.

Its objective is to ensure that the final system satisfies mission requirements while remaining affordable, supportable, and technically feasible.

## Core Principles

Systems engineering emphasizes:

- requirements analysis
- architecture development
- decomposition
- integration
- verification
- validation
- continuous technical assessment

---

## Verification vs Validation

Verification determines:

> Did we build the system correctly?

Validation determines:

> Did we build the correct system?

Verification focuses on engineering requirements.

Validation focuses on operational needs.

---

## Systems Engineering Triangle

The systems engineering process can be viewed as three major activities.

### Decomposition

Breaking high-level operational needs into progressively lower-level technical requirements.

---

### Realization

Designing, building, and implementing the system.

---

### Integration

Combining individual components into a complete operational system while verifying compatibility and performance.

---

# Technical Planning

Technical planning defines the scope of engineering work required throughout the program.

It establishes:

- technical tasks
- schedules
- resources
- engineering milestones
- risk mitigation strategies

The Systems Engineering Plan (SEP) is a living document that evolves throughout the program rather than being submitted only once.

---

# Testing and Evaluation

Testing provides objective evidence that the system satisfies both engineering requirements and operational needs.

Testing progresses from laboratory evaluations to operational use.

## Developmental Test and Evaluation (DT&E)

Measures technical performance against engineering specifications.

DT&E supports systems engineering by identifying deficiencies before production.

---

## Operational Assessment (OA)

Provides an early operational evaluation, often using prototype systems.

The purpose is to identify deficiencies before Initial Operational Test and Evaluation.

---

## Initial Operational Test and Evaluation (IOT&E)

Determines whether the system is operationally effective and suitable before full-rate production.

---

## Follow-On Operational Test and Evaluation (FOT&E)

Continues testing after fielding to ensure long-term operational effectiveness.

---

## Test and Evaluation Master Plan (TEMP)

The TEMP documents the overall strategy for developmental and operational testing.

For major acquisition programs, it is a required planning document.

---

# Cost Estimation

Accurate cost estimation supports acquisition decisions throughout program development.

Several estimating techniques are available depending on program maturity.

## Engineering Estimates

Most detailed.

Used when sufficient design information exists.

---

## Analogy Estimates

Based upon similar historical systems.

Useful during early concept development.

---

## Parametric Estimates

Use mathematical relationships between system characteristics and historical costs.

Appropriate when:

- many comparable systems exist
- rapid estimates are required
- moderate uncertainty is acceptable

---

## Life Cycle Cost Estimate (LCCE)

The LCCE estimates the total ownership cost of a system across its entire life cycle.

It includes:

- development
- procurement
- operations
- maintenance
- disposal

These estimates support funding decisions and affordability analyses.

---

# Budgeting and Appropriations

Different appropriations support different phases of the acquisition life cycle.

## Research, Development, Test and Evaluation (RDT&E)

Funds:

- research
- development
- engineering
- testing

---

## Procurement

Funds production of approved systems.

---

## Operations and Maintenance (O&M)

Funds operation and sustainment of fielded systems.

---

## Military Personnel (MILPERS)

Funds military pay and benefits.

---

## Military Construction (MILCON)

Funds military infrastructure projects.

---

# Budget Authority

Budget Authority (BA) provides legal authority to incur financial obligations.

Movement of BA within established limits is known as **realignment**.

Changing Congressional intent requires **reprogramming**, which involves higher-level approval and, in some cases, Congressional action.

---

# Contracting

Federal acquisition is governed by competition, legal authority, and accountability.

## Competition

Federal agencies generally use **full and open competition** unless otherwise authorized by law.

---

## Contracting Officer

Only the Contracting Officer possesses authority to:

- award contracts
- modify contracts
- obligate Government funds

---

## Contract Administration

Contract administration ensures contracts are executed properly while avoiding duplication of Government oversight efforts.

---

## Requirements Documentation

Typical acquisition documentation includes:

- Specifications
- Statement of Work (SOW)
- Contract Data Requirements
- Technical Data Packages

These documents communicate engineering requirements to industry.

---

# Contract Types

Contract selection depends upon technical maturity and risk.

## Fixed-Price Contracts

Most financial risk is placed upon the contractor.

Best suited for stable, well-defined requirements.

---

## Cost-Reimbursement Contracts

Greater financial risk is assumed by the Government.

Typically used when technical uncertainty is high.

---

## Time-and-Materials Contracts

Government pays for labor and materials.

Appropriate when scope cannot be accurately estimated.

---

## Indefinite Delivery Contracts

Provide contractual flexibility for future task or delivery orders.

---

# Technical Data Package

The Technical Data Package (TDP) defines how the system is manufactured.

Typical contents include:

- engineering drawings
- specifications
- parts lists
- manufacturing processes
- material specifications

---

# Sustainment and Logistics

Modern acquisition emphasizes designing supportability into the system from the beginning rather than treating logistics as an afterthought.

## Life Cycle Logistics

Life Cycle Logistics develops affordable product support strategies that maximize readiness throughout the system's service life.

Primary goals include:

- availability
- reliability
- maintainability
- affordability
- supportability

---

## Product Support Strategy

Documents how sustainment will be implemented throughout the system life cycle.

---

## Integrated Product Support (IPS)

Major IPS elements include:

- manpower and personnel
- training
- supply support
- maintenance planning
- technical data
- product support management

---

## Availability

Availability is primarily influenced by:

- reliability
- maintainability

These two characteristics largely determine overall system readiness.

---

# Performance Based Logistics (PBL)

Performance Based Logistics shifts emphasis from purchasing maintenance activities to purchasing measurable performance outcomes.

Rather than paying for individual repairs, incentives encourage suppliers to maximize operational availability while minimizing total ownership cost.

Successful PBL arrangements:

- align Government and contractor incentives
- use measurable performance metrics
- focus on warfighter outcomes
- encourage continuous improvement

---

# Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity protects information systems throughout the acquisition life cycle.

Core objectives include:

- confidentiality
- integrity
- availability
- authentication
- nonrepudiation

Cybersecurity encompasses preventing damage, protecting, and restoring computers, communication systems, and electronic information.

---

# Intelligence and Counterintelligence

Acquisition programs must protect Critical Program Information (CPI) against foreign intelligence threats.

Counterintelligence supports acquisition by:

- identifying foreign collection threats
- assessing vulnerabilities
- informing acquisition strategy
- protecting sensitive technologies

Within DoD, centralized Counterintelligence oversight resides within the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence.

The Intelligence Community itself is coordinated by the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI), led by the Director of National Intelligence (DNI).

---

# Change Management

Acquisition programs constantly evolve as technology, threats, and requirements change.

Formal **change management** is a discrete approval process used to control modifications to program baselines.

Successfully **managing change**, however, is a continuous activity requiring ongoing communication, stakeholder involvement, and adaptation throughout the program life cycle.

---

# Integrated Acquisition Philosophy

Several common themes appear throughout modern DoD acquisition.

Programs should:

- begin sustainment planning early
- reduce technical risk before production
- verify designs through testing
- validate operational effectiveness with users
- integrate logistics throughout development
- manage affordability across the entire life cycle
- make decisions using objective evidence rather than assumptions
- optimize total system performance rather than individual components

The acquisition process therefore represents a balance between engineering, management, budgeting, contracting, logistics, testing, and operational effectiveness—all working together to deliver capability to the warfighter.
